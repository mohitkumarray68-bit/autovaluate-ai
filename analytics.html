Key Engineering Principles

Why standard machine learning pipelines fail on automobile pricing and how this system solves it

01

Logarithmic Target Stabilization

Automobile selling prices are heavily right-skewed: the majority of cars trade between ₹2.5 L and ₹8.5 L, while top-tier SUVs and German luxury cars command ₹40 L to ₹1 Cr+. Training directly on raw rupees forces regression loss functions to obsess over million-rupee luxury residuals at the expense of commuter cars. By transforming the target to np.log1p(selling_price), errors become relative percentages rather than absolute currency errors.

02

OEM Spec Cross-Referencing

Unlike naive forms that expect users to memorize their car's exact displacement (CC) and brake horsepower (bhp), our platform indexes manufacturer catalogs across 31 brands and 150+ models. When a user selects a vehicle, typical OEM specifications are automatically populated while still permitting fine-tuning for aftermarket or variant differences.

03

Non-Linear Depreciation Trajectories

Vehicles do not depreciate in a straight line. The initial 3 years incur steep 15–20% annual losses, tapering into a flatter curve after year 6. The 150-tree Random Forest natively captures these non-linear interaction bends across age, mileage brackets, and brand retention characteristics.
